Arizona metal

Arizona metal history

From a Phoenix high school talent show in 1964 to Billboard chart runs and world tours: the bands, clubs and legends that made the desert one of the heaviest places in America.

The timeline

How Arizona got heavy

  • 1964 · PhoenixA 16-year-old Vincent Furnier puts together a band for the Cortez High School talent show. They go from the Earwigs to the Spiders to Nazz, and by 1968 the band and the singer are both called Alice Cooper.Sources: Wikipedia; Phoenix New Times
  • Early 1980s · The Valley's first waveLocal bands like Surgical Steel and the School Boys pack the bars and set the stage for everything that follows. The Mason Jar, run by Franco Gagliano from 1980 to 2005, becomes the club every heavy band in town plays. Today that room is The Rebel Lounge.Sources: Phoenix New Times; East Valley Tribune
  • Early 1980s · Paradox to FlotsamA Phoenix band cycles through the names Paradox, Dredlox and Dogz before settling on Flotsam and Jetsam in 1984, with Jason Newsted on bass. Local players called them the scene's “big brother band.”Sources: Wikipedia; Phoenix New Times
  • 1983 · The BootleggerGloria Cavalera and her brother Paul Guffin open The Bootlegger at 36th Street and Thunderbird Road, remembered as Arizona's first all-ages rock and metal bar. Rob Halford was a regular. It was eventually shut down for being too rowdy.Sources: Phoenix New Times; Kerrang!
  • Mid-1980s · The Metal God moves inJudas Priest singer Rob Halford makes Phoenix his home, and the Valley has claimed him ever since.Sources: Phoenix New Times; Kerrang!
  • 1985 · Sacred ReichSacred Reich forms at Coronado High School in Scottsdale. Their debut, “Ignorance,” follows on Metal Blade Records in October 1987, and in 1989 their Dynamo Open Air set in the Netherlands becomes the live album “Alive at the Dynamo.”Sources: Wikipedia; Phoenix New Times
  • 1986 · Phoenix to MetallicaFlotsam and Jetsam release “Doomsday for the Deceiver” on Metal Blade on July 4. After Cliff Burton dies in September, Metallica hires Jason Newsted, and he plays his last Flotsam show on Halloween night.Sources: Wikipedia; Phoenix New Times; Kerrang!
  • Late 1980s to early 1990s · Legends in the ValleyDave Mustaine runs Megadeth out of Phoenix for a stretch, Slayer's Kerry King lives here too, and Max Cavalera moves from Brazil to Phoenix in the early '90s.Sources: Phoenix New Times (two articles)
  • 1997 · SoulflyAfter leaving Sepultura, Max Cavalera forms Soulfly in Phoenix.Sources: Wikipedia; Phoenix New Times
  • 2003 · GlendaleJob For A Cowboy forms in Glendale in December. Their debut album “Genesis” reaches No. 54 on the Billboard 200 in 2007, and in 2024 “Moon Healer” climbs to No. 29, their highest chart spot yet.Sources: Wikipedia; KLAQ; Phoenix New Times
  • 2012 · Giving backAlice Cooper's Solid Rock foundation opens its first teen center in Phoenix, and a second follows in Mesa in 2021.Source: Wikipedia
  • 2010s to now · The new bloodGatecreeper, which Kerrang! calls Arizona's most identifiable underground band, carries the desert's death metal worldwide, while Phoenix New Times names Vehemence the godfathers of Arizona metal. Flotsam and Jetsam are still going, with “Rats in the Temple” out on Napalm Records in August 2026.Sources: Kerrang!; Phoenix New Times; Wikipedia

They called Arizona home

Legends of the desert

Arizona has been home to Alice Cooper, Rob Halford, Max Cavalera, Jason Newsted and members of Sacred Reich, and at different times to Dave Mustaine, Kerry King and George Lynch. Linkin Park's Chester Bennington was born in Phoenix. SiriusXM Liquid Metal's Jose Mangin grew up in Douglas.

Sources: Phoenix New Times; Kerrang!; KLAQ.
