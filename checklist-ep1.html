Band Survival Kit · Episode 1 checklist

Finding band members

From “Wanted: Screamer.” Use this before anyone new joins your band.

1. Know what you need

  • Style and influences you’re going for
  • Gear they need to bring (and who pays for it)
  • Practice schedule and how often you’ll play shows
  • How serious the band is: hobby, part-time, or full send

2. Look where musicians are

  • Local music groups and musician-wanted posts online
  • Rehearsal space and music store bulletin boards
  • Weeknight local shows: talk to people after the set
  • Friends of other bands you play with

3. Run a real tryout

  • Send the same two songs to everyone ahead of time
  • Play together, then hang out together
  • Ask everyone the same questions: Can you make practice? What gear do you have? Are you in other bands? What do you want from this band?

4. Agree on the basics, in writing

  • How you split money, and how much goes to a band fund
  • Who pays for what (gas, rehearsal space, recording)
  • Who owns the band name
  • Who owns the songs you write
  • What happens if someone leaves (gear, money, the name, the songs)
General education, not legal advice. For a full band agreement, have a lawyer look it over.
